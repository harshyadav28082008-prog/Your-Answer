CHEMISTRY · ACIDS AND BASES

Acid–Base and pH

Explore the pH scale, observe indicator colours, and investigate how acids and bases neutralise one another.

1. Explore the pH scale

For dilute aqueous solutions at about 25°C, pH below 7 is acidic, pH 7 is neutral, and pH above 7 is alkaline.

3. Neutralisation simulation

Add acid and base to a model mixture. The simulation tracks equivalent amounts of acid and base, assuming a simple strong-acid/strong-base reaction with equal concentrations. It is a conceptual model, not a laboratory-grade pH calculator.

4. Procedure

  1. Move the pH slider across the scale.
  2. Compare the colours of different indicators.
  3. Reset the mixture, then add acid and base.
  4. Observe what happens when their amounts become equal.
  5. Record your findings in the table.

5. Key ideas

  • Acids increase hydronium ion concentration in water.
  • Bases reduce hydronium concentration or increase hydroxide concentration.
  • Neutralisation commonly produces water and a salt.
  • Equal volumes do not always neutralise each other in real experiments; concentration and reaction stoichiometry matter.
