Hooke’s Law

Apply a force to a spring and investigate how its extension depends on the force and the spring constant.

1. Experiment controls

\(F = kx\)

F = applied force, k = spring constant, and x = extension from the spring’s natural length.

2. Spring model

Extension (x)0.200 m
Extension in cm20.0 cm
Spring constant (k)25 N/m
Spring force (kx)5.0 N

3. Force versus extension

The straight line shows the predicted relationship for an ideal spring. The marker represents your current settings.

4. Procedure

  1. Choose a spring constant using the slider.
  2. Increase the applied force in small steps.
  3. Record the extension for each force.
  4. Repeat with a different spring constant.
  5. Compare the graph gradients and observations.

5. What to investigate

  • What happens to extension when force doubles?
  • Which spring stretches more under the same force?
  • What does the graph’s gradient represent?

In this ideal model, the spring obeys Hooke’s law throughout the displayed range. Real springs can stop behaving linearly if stretched too far.
