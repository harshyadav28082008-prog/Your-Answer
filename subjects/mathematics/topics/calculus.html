Calculus: Change, Made Understandable.

Calculus gives us a language for understanding how things change. Start with the idea of a rate of change, explore derivatives, and see how a curve behaves at a particular point.

Beginner foundations Interactive explorer Approx. 15 minutes
Overview Derivatives Worked example Explore Practice

1. What is calculus?

Imagine a car travelling along a road. Its position changes with time, and its speed tells us how quickly that position changes. Calculus studies ideas like these, including situations where change is not constant.

Two big ideas

Differentiation studies instantaneous rates of change and the slopes of curves.

Integration studies accumulation, such as the total distance travelled from a changing velocity.

This lesson focuses on differentiation. Integration will be explored separately as you progress.

2. Understanding derivatives

A derivative measures how rapidly a function changes at a particular input. Geometrically, it represents the slope of the tangent line to a curve at that point.

\(f'(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{h\to0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\)

This is the limit definition of a derivative. It compares the change in the function with a small change in its input, then considers what happens as that input change approaches zero.

A useful rule

If \(f(x)=x^n\), then \(f'(x)=nx^{n-1}\)

For powers of \(x\), multiply by the exponent and reduce the exponent by one. This rule works for positive integer powers and has broader extensions.

3. A worked example

Find the derivative of the function:

\(f(x)=x^2+3x+2\)
1 Differentiate \(x^2\). The power rule gives \(2x\).
2 Differentiate \(3x\). Its derivative is \(3\).
3 The derivative of a constant is zero, so the derivative of \(2\) is \(0\).
\(f'(x)=2x+3\)

At \(x=2\), the derivative is \(f'(2)=2(2)+3=7\). Therefore, the tangent line to this curve at \(x=2\) has slope \(7\).
