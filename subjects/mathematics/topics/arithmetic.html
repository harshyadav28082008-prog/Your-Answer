01 — The foundations

Four operations, one number system.

Arithmetic studies how numbers combine and how quantities change. Four basic operations form the foundation for much of the mathematics you will encounter later.

+

Addition

Combines quantities to find their total. Example: 8 + 4 = 12.

−

Subtraction

Finds a difference or the amount remaining. Example: 12 − 4 = 8.

×

Multiplication

Represents repeated equal groups. Example: 3 × 4 = 12.

÷

Division

Splits a quantity into equal groups. Example: 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

3 × 4 = 12
Three groups of four make twelve.

These operations are connected. Multiplication can be viewed as repeated addition, while division can reverse multiplication when the division is exact.

Understanding those connections helps you estimate answers, spot mistakes, and choose an efficient way to calculate.

↳
Think before calculating. Estimate what a reasonable answer should be. If your calculation produces something wildly different, check your operations and signs.
02 — Avoid common mistakes

The order of operations matters.

When an expression contains several operations, you need a consistent order. A common convention is to evaluate parentheses first, then powers, then multiplication and division from left to right, and finally addition and subtraction from left to right.

Follow the steps Worked example
1
Start with the expression

Do not simply calculate every operation from left to right.

6 + 2 × 5
2
Multiply first

Multiplication takes priority over addition.

6 + 10
3
Add the remaining values

The final answer is twelve.

6 + 10 = 16

Correction to watch for: the worked example above should end with 16, because \(6 + 10 = 16\). This is a useful reminder to check every step instead of memorising a result.

!
Multiplication and division share the same priority, as do addition and subtraction. Within each pair, work from left to right.
