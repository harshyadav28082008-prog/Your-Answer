See the mathematics of space.

Geometry explores shapes, sizes, angles, and the space around us. Learn how to describe figures, measure their boundaries, and calculate the area they cover.

01 — The foundations

Shapes tell us about space.

Geometry gives us a mathematical language for describing objects and spaces. A shape can have edges, vertices, angles, curved boundaries, or a combination of these features.

Start with a few familiar two-dimensional shapes.

□

Square

Four equal sides and four right angles.

▭

Rectangle

Opposite sides are equal and all angles are right angles.

△

Triangle

Three sides. Its interior angles add up to 180°.

○

Circle

A curved boundary whose points are equally distant from its centre.

↳
Look for structure. A shape is not defined only by its appearance. Its sides, angles, symmetry, and measurements help us describe it precisely.
02 — Measuring shapes

Perimeter is not area.

Two measurements are especially useful. Perimeter measures the distance around a two-dimensional shape. Area measures the amount of surface enclosed by its boundary.

Worked example: a rectangle 4 by 3 units
1
Identify the dimensions

The rectangle has length 4 units and width 3 units.

l = 4, w = 3
2
Calculate the perimeter

Add all four sides, or double the sum of length and width.

P = 2(4 + 3) = 14
3
Calculate the area

Multiply the length by the width.

A = 4 × 3 = 12
A = l × w
Rectangle area = length × width

The rectangle's perimeter is 14 units, while its area is 12 square units. Notice that perimeter and area use different kinds of units: ordinary units for length and square units for area.

!
A perimeter answers “how far around?” An area answers “how much surface?” Never confuse the two.

Keep learning
