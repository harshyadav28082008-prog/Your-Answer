Mathematics / Beyond the Foundations

Advanced mathematics: discover the deeper structure.

Move beyond individual formulas and explore the structures, patterns, and methods that connect different branches of mathematics. This page is a guided map of topics to study next.

Intermediate to advanced Conceptual guide Prerequisites included

01. A roadmap for advanced mathematics

Advanced mathematics is not one subject. It is a collection of connected fields, each with its own questions, language, and tools. Build a solid foundation before moving into proofs and abstract structures.

Suggested learning order

  1. Strengthen algebra, functions, and trigonometry.
  2. Learn limits, derivatives, integrals, and series.
  3. Study vectors, matrices, and systems of equations.
  4. Practise logic, sets, and mathematical proofs.
  5. Choose a field and explore its deeper questions.

02. Linear algebra

Linear algebra studies vectors, matrices, linear transformations, and systems of linear equations. It is central to physics, computer graphics, data science, and machine learning.

Vectors

A vector can represent a quantity with components and direction.

\[ \mathbf{v}=\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix} \]

Matrix multiplication

Matrices can represent transformations that rotate, stretch, reflect, or otherwise map vectors to new vectors.

\[ A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b} \]
Example

Consider the system:

\[ x+y=5,\qquad x-y=1 \]

Adding the equations gives \(2x=6\), so \(x=3\). Substituting gives \(y=2\).

03. Differential equations

A differential equation relates an unknown function to one or more of its derivatives. These equations model change in mechanics, electrical circuits, population models, and many other systems.

A simple example

\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=ky \]

This equation says that the rate of change of \(y\) is proportional to its current value. Its general solution is:

\[ y=Ce^{kx} \]

Here \(C\) is a constant determined by an initial condition, and \(k\) controls the growth or decay behaviour.

04. Number theory

Number theory explores integers and their properties. It asks questions about divisibility, prime numbers, remainders, and patterns in whole numbers.

Modular arithmetic

Modular arithmetic studies remainders after division. For example, \(17\) leaves remainder \(5\) when divided by \(6\).

\[ 17\equiv5\pmod 6 \]

This notation is useful for clock arithmetic, cyclic patterns, and some areas of cryptography.

05. Mathematical logic and proofs

Mathematical logic studies statements and the rules used to reason about them. Proofs establish why a claim must be true under specified assumptions.

Implication

\[ P\Rightarrow Q \]

This means “if \(P\), then \(Q\).” A proof must establish the conclusion from the assumptions, not merely show that a few examples work.

Common proof methods

  • Direct proof: derive the conclusion from the assumptions.
  • Contrapositive: prove that not-\(Q\) implies not-\(P\).
  • Contradiction: show that denying the claim leads to an inconsistency.
  • Induction: prove a base case and then a step connecting successive cases.

06. Sequences and infinite series

A sequence is an ordered list of terms. An infinite series adds the terms of a sequence, and its behaviour depends on whether the partial sums approach a finite limit.

Geometric series

\[ 1+r+r^2+\cdots+r^n =\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r},\quad r\ne1 \]

For an infinite geometric series, the sum is finite when \(|r|<1\):

\[ 1+r+r^2+\cdots=\frac{1}{1-r} \quad\text{when }|r|<1 \]

07. Choose your next field

Once the foundations are strong, choose a field based on the questions you want to investigate.

For physics and the universe

Explore multivariable calculus, differential equations, vector calculus, and linear algebra.

For probability and prediction

Explore combinatorics, probability distributions, statistical inference, and stochastic processes.

For abstract patterns

Explore number theory, abstract algebra, mathematical logic, and set theory.

For computation

Explore numerical methods, discrete mathematics, algorithms, and optimisation.

08. Quick practice

Question 1

If \(x+y=9\) and \(x-y=3\), find \(x\) and \(y\).

Reveal solution

Add the equations: \(2x=12\), so \(x=6\). Then \(y=3\).

Question 2

What is the remainder when \(23\) is divided by \(7\)?

Reveal solution

\(23=3(7)+2\), so the remainder is \(2\). In modular notation, \(23\equiv2\pmod7\).

Question 3

Find the infinite sum \(1+\frac12+\frac14+\frac18+\cdots\).

Reveal solution

This is a geometric series with \(r=\frac12\). Its sum is \(\frac{1}{1-\frac12}=2\).
