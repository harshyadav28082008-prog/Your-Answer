Waves & Oscillations

Explore periodic motion, wave behaviour, wavelength, frequency, sound, resonance, and the patterns created when waves interact.

Oscillations

An oscillation is a repeated motion around an equilibrium position. A swinging pendulum and a vibrating spring are familiar examples.

Amplitude The maximum displacement from the equilibrium position.
Period The time required for one complete cycle.
Frequency The number of complete cycles occurring per unit time.

Period and frequency

Period and frequency describe the same repeating behaviour from two different perspectives.

Relationship

f = 1 / T

Frequency f is measured in hertz (Hz), while period T is measured in seconds.

Simple harmonic motion

Simple harmonic motion is a special type of oscillation in which the restoring acceleration is proportional to the displacement from equilibrium and directed toward equilibrium.

Angular frequency

ω = 2πf = 2π / T

Angular frequency describes how quickly the phase of an oscillation changes.

What is a wave?

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy and information from one location to another. The particles of a medium do not necessarily travel with the wave over the same distance as the disturbance itself.

Transverse wave The oscillation is perpendicular to the direction in which the wave propagates.
Longitudinal wave The oscillation is parallel to the direction of propagation.

Wave speed

Wave speed connects wavelength and frequency. A wave with a larger frequency can travel at the same speed if its wavelength is correspondingly smaller.

Wave equation

v = fλ

v is wave speed, f is frequency, and λ is wavelength.

Sound waves

Sound is a mechanical wave produced by oscillating matter. In ordinary conditions, sound requires a material medium through which to propagate.

In air, sound travels mainly as a longitudinal pressure wave. Its speed depends on properties of the medium and its temperature.

Superposition

When waves overlap, their displacements combine. This principle allows waves to interfere constructively or destructively.

Constructive interference Overlapping disturbances reinforce one another, producing a larger resultant amplitude.
Destructive interference Overlapping disturbances partially or completely cancel.

Resonance

Resonance occurs when a system is driven at or near one of its natural frequencies. Under suitable conditions, the amplitude of the resulting oscillation can become much larger.

Resonance appears in mechanical systems, sound, electrical circuits, and many other physical systems.

Standing waves

Standing waves can form when waves travelling in opposite directions interfere with one another.

Node A point where the displacement remains zero in the ideal standing-wave pattern.
Antinode A point where the oscillation reaches maximum amplitude.

Quick example

Apply the idea

A wave has a frequency of 20 Hz and a wavelength of 3 m. What is its speed?

Using:

v = fλ

v = 20 × 3

Answer: The wave travels at 60 m/s.
